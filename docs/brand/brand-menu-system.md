# MajdaMatcha Brand and Menu System

## Launch Positioning

MajdaMatcha is a premium Saudi matcha house built around a simple idea: the quiet discipline of Japanese matcha, served through the warmth, generosity, and social rhythm of Saudi hospitality.

The brand should feel polished but not cold, feminine but not fragile, and local without turning into a heritage pastiche. It belongs in Riyadh, Jeddah, Khobar, and destination retail environments where guests expect taste, service, design, and cultural intelligence to arrive together.

MajdaMatcha is not a generic cafe with green drinks. It is a matcha-first ritual brand for people who care about quality, presentation, and the small ceremony of meeting someone properly.

## Brand Story

In Saudi homes, the best moments often begin with a cup placed in the hand before anything else is asked. MajdaMatcha carries that instinct into a new form: finely whisked matcha, thoughtful sweets, and modern Saudi hosting.

The brand imagines matcha not as an imported trend, but as a ritual that can sit naturally beside dates, cardamom, rose, saffron, sesame, honey, citrus, and the evening pace of the Gulf. Every drink should feel composed. Every box should feel giftable. Every store should feel like a calm pause inside a busy day.

MajdaMatcha is for the friend meeting after Asr, the bride choosing elegant favors, the office treating a team, the shopper taking a quiet break, and the guest who wants something lighter than coffee but more intentional than juice.

## Name Rationale

Majda means glory, nobility, and distinction in Arabic. It has a confident Saudi resonance without feeling heavy or old-fashioned. Paired with Matcha, the name becomes clear, memorable, and category-owning.

The repetition of the "ma" sound makes the name soft and easy to say. "MajdaMatcha" should be treated as a single modern mark: elevated, feminine, and precise.

Arabic rendering guidance:

- Primary English: MajdaMatcha
- Arabic display option: ماجدة ماتشا
- Short social handle option: Majda Matcha
- Avoid: "Majda's Matcha", "Majda Cafe", or overly playful spellings that weaken the premium tone.

## Mission

To make matcha a beautiful part of Saudi daily hospitality through exceptional drinks, refined gifting, and flavors that respect both Japanese craft and local taste.

## Brand Values

- Craft before trend: matcha quality, water temperature, whisking, texture, and balance matter.
- Saudi hospitality: guests are welcomed with grace, not scripts.
- Modern femininity: elegant, capable, composed, and quietly expressive.
- Local fluency: Saudi ingredients and occasions are built into the menu, not added as decoration.
- Giftability: anything leaving the store should feel worthy of being handed to someone.

## Brand Voice

MajdaMatcha speaks with warmth, restraint, and sensory detail. The voice is confident without over-explaining, premium without sounding distant, and culturally aware without forced references.

Use:

- "Whisked to order."
- "A softer afternoon."
- "Saffron, date, and matcha in balance."
- "For the table, for the office, for the majlis."
- "Prepared with ceremony. Served with ease."

Avoid:

- Generic wellness claims like "detox", "clean energy", or "guilt-free".
- Overused cafe phrases like "sip, relax, repeat" or "your new obsession".
- Imported Japanese cliches used decoratively.
- Casual slang that will date quickly.
- Religious or national language used as marketing garnish.

## Logo and Wordmark Direction

The wordmark should be custom or lightly customized, not a stock cafe script. It should balance Arabic softness with Japanese precision.

Direction:

- Use a refined serif or high-contrast sans-serif for "Majda" paired with a cleaner, quieter "Matcha".
- Consider a custom ligature or joined rhythm between the two words, especially around the repeated "Ma".
- Arabic companion mark should be treated as a serious parallel asset, not a translation tucked under the English.
- Icon direction can reference a matcha whisk stroke, crescent-like bowl curve, or date palm rib line, but should remain abstract and premium.
- Avoid cartoon cups, bamboo clip art, leaves in every letter, geisha references, or overly literal Japanese motifs.

Logo system:

- Primary: MajdaMatcha wordmark
- Bilingual lockup: MajdaMatcha / ماجدة ماتشا
- Small mark: abstract whisk-bowl monogram using M/M or a curved whisk stroke
- Seal: circular gift seal for packaging, catering, and Eid/Ramadan boxes

## Typography Direction

English:

- Premium editorial serif for headlines, such as Canela, IvyPresto, Cormorant Garamond, or a similar high-contrast serif.
- Clean humanist sans for menus and operations, such as Neue Haas Grotesk, Avenir Next, Satoshi, or Inter.

Arabic:

- Elegant Arabic headline face with calligraphic warmth, such as IBM Plex Sans Arabic, TheSans Arabic, DIN Next Arabic, or a custom Naskh-inspired direction.
- Arabic menu text must be highly legible at small sizes. Do not choose decorative Arabic type for item descriptions.

Hierarchy:

- Wordmark: custom.
- Headlines: refined serif or Arabic equivalent.
- Menu items: clean sans, generous line height.
- Prices: tabular numerals where possible.
- Bilingual layouts: Arabic should not feel secondary; balance scale and spacing.

## Photography and Art Direction

Photography should feel sunlit, tactile, and composed. The visual world is premium Saudi daily life, not anonymous wellness imagery.

Scenes to capture:

- Matcha being whisked by hand, with foam texture visible.
- A chilled matcha poured over ice and milk in a tall glass.
- Dates, saffron threads, rose petals, sesame, citrus peel, and honey as supporting details.
- Small tables with two or three cups, suggesting conversation.
- Gift boxes held in hands, placed on a car seat, or carried into a home.
- Ramadan evening trays, Eid gifting, office catering, and bridal favor moments.

Lighting:

- Natural Gulf daylight, soft shadows, warm stone, porcelain, brushed metal, and pale wood.
- Evening content can use lantern warmth but should stay modern.

Avoid:

- Dark moody coffee-shop photography.
- Stock hands, fake laughter, cluttered tabletops, and generic laptop cafe scenes.
- Overcrowded ingredient flat lays.
- Cultural props used without a real hosting context.

## Store Experience Notes

The store should feel like a calm matcha atelier with Saudi hospitality embedded in service flow.

Arrival:

- Guests should see whisking or pouring within the first sightline.
- A small tasting tray can introduce first-time guests to classic matcha, date, and saffron profiles.
- Staff language should be graceful and direct: "Would you prefer it soft, creamy, or more matcha-forward?"

Spatial tone:

- Warm stone, matcha green accents, porcelain, brushed brass, and pale wood.
- Seating should support short visits, pairs, and gift-order waiting.
- A packaging wall or retail shelf should feel like a boutique, not a grab-and-go rack.

Service rituals:

- Whisk signature drinks to order where operationally possible.
- Offer sweetness levels: unsweetened, light, house, Saudi sweet.
- Offer milk choices: fresh milk, oat, coconut, almond.
- Offer hot, iced, and cloud options where each item supports it.
- During Ramadan, shift the rhythm toward post-iftar evening service, gift boxes, and majlis trays.

## Menu Strategy

Menu principles:

- Keep matcha quality visible and explainable.
- Use Saudi-localized flavors as serious signatures, not novelty items.
- Offer enough non-matcha options for groups, while keeping matcha as the hero.
- Price as premium casual: accessible for daily visits, elevated for signatures and gifting.
- Keep names elegant and easy to say in English and Arabic contexts.

## Operational Notes

- Train baristas on matcha grams, water temperature, sift quality, whisk timing, and texture standards.
- Clearly label caffeine level for matcha and coffee.
- Offer sweetness choices but set "house" sweetness as balanced, not overly sweet.
- Use premium milk and oat milk; weak milk quality will flatten the menu.
- Keep seasonal drinks limited and calendar-based so they feel intentional.
- Every item should photograph cleanly from the top and side for social sharing.
- Menus should be bilingual where possible, with Arabic treated as part of the design system.

## Cultural Awareness Notes

- Saudi flavors should be used with respect and restraint: date, saffron, cardamom, rose, mint, citrus, pistachio, sesame, honey, and qamar al-din all have natural fit.
- Ramadan and Eid offers should support gathering and gifting, not reduce the occasions to decorative campaigns.
- Avoid alcohol references in dessert language. For example, "matcha tiramisu cup" should be clearly alcohol-free if needed.
- Be mindful of sweetness preferences while still preserving matcha character.
- Build hospitality into the service script, packaging, and gifting, not just the menu names.
