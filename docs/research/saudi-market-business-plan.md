# MajdaMatcha Saudi Market & Business Plan

## Executive View

Saudi Arabia is attractive for a premium matcha launch because three forces overlap: fast-growing F&B spending, a mature cafe-going culture, and a clear recent spike in matcha demand. Knight Frank reported Saudi consumer spending reached SAR 1.41 trillion in 2024, with restaurant and cafe spending making up 29% of POS transactions in Q1 2025. Market researchers also estimate Saudi foodservice at roughly USD 27.18 billion in 2024, growing toward USD 45.92 billion by 2033. Matcha is no longer a niche wellness import: Arab News, citing Al-Eqtisadiah, reported Japanese matcha imports into Saudi Arabia rose from 9,000 kg in 2022 to 81,000 kg in 2023, then 46,000 kg worth SAR 7 million in 2024.

MajdaMatcha should launch as a premium, Saudi-localized matcha brand: Japanese-grade tea quality, Arabic hospitality, modern cafe convenience, and visual presentation made for social sharing without becoming a gimmick. The recommended launch format is a Riyadh-first pop-up and delivery-led micro-cafe, followed by a compact flagship or shop-in-shop once demand, margins, and neighborhood fit are proven.

## Target Customers

**Primary customers**

- Women and men aged 18-35 in Riyadh and Jeddah who already buy premium coffee, iced drinks, dessert drinks, and wellness-adjacent beverages.
- University students and early-career professionals looking for a lighter caffeine ritual than coffee.
- Social cafe users who value atmosphere, packaging, limited drops, and photogenic drinks.
- Health-conscious consumers interested in antioxidants, lower-acidity caffeine, oat milk, reduced sugar, and ingredient transparency.

**Secondary customers**

- Office teams and event buyers for branded beverage bars, catering trays, and corporate wellness activations.
- Tourists and residents visiting lifestyle districts, malls, gyms, salons, concept stores, and cultural events.
- Home ritual buyers purchasing matcha tins, starter kits, whisks, cups, syrups, and subscription refills.

## Positioning

**Positioning statement:** MajdaMatcha is Saudi Arabia's approachable premium matcha ritual: high-quality matcha, beautiful cold drinks, soft Saudi hospitality, and a menu designed for everyday indulgence rather than occasional novelty.

**Brand territory**

- Premium but not intimidating.
- Wellness-coded but still delicious.
- Saudi-localized without losing Japanese tea credibility.
- Social-friendly without relying only on trend aesthetics.

**Menu anchors**

- Iced Majda Matcha Latte.
- Strawberry, mango, rose, pistachio, date, and honey matcha variants.
- Unsweetened and low-sugar options.
- Oat milk and lactose-free milk as default upgrades.
- Small dessert pairings: matcha cookies, mochi-inspired bites, date truffles, banana bread, and soft-serve or affogato-style specials.

## Market Opportunity

Saudi foodservice has structural support from Vision 2030 lifestyle, tourism, entertainment, mall, and mixed-use development. Multiple market estimates point to mid-to-high single-digit foodservice growth through 2030-2034, with urban F&B concentrated in Riyadh, Jeddah, and the Eastern Province. The Saudi Tourism Ministry's 2024 statistics, reported by regional business media, showed 116 million domestic and international tourists and SAR 284 billion in tourism spending, widening the audience for lifestyle F&B brands in destination districts.

Matcha demand has a particularly strong near-term signal. Arab News reported Saudi Japanese matcha imports grew almost 900% in 2023, with continued 2024 demand and cafe examples where matcha lattes reached meaningful sales share. This creates a window for a focused matcha brand before the category becomes fully commoditized by coffee chains and dessert cafes.

The opportunity is not to out-scale Starbucks or large Saudi coffee chains immediately. It is to own a sharper niche: the most trusted, beautiful, and locally resonant matcha brand for Saudi consumers who want cafe-quality matcha as a regular habit.

## Launch City Assumptions

**Recommended first city: Riyadh**

Riyadh should be the first launch city because it concentrates high-income consumers, offices, lifestyle districts, entertainment seasons, specialty coffee competition, and social discovery. It is also likely to provide the strongest test of premium pricing and repeat consumption.

**Initial neighborhood shortlist**

- North Riyadh lifestyle and office corridors.
- Boulevard / Riyadh Season-adjacent activations where short-term pop-ups can create visibility.
- Mall or concept-store shop-in-shop in a premium footfall location.
- University and gym-adjacent areas for weekday repeat demand.

**Second city: Jeddah**

Jeddah is the natural second market because of its strong cafe culture, coastal lifestyle, design-forward F&B scene, and tourism relevance. Jeddah can be used for seasonal pop-ups and brand collaborations before a permanent store.

## Competitive Landscape

**Competitor categories**

- Global coffee chains: Starbucks, Costa, Dunkin, and international hotel cafes. Strengths are reach and convenience; weakness is less specialized matcha credibility.
- Saudi specialty coffee chains and roasters: Half Million, Barn's, Elixir Bunn, Camel Step, Brew92, Overdose, and local independents. Strengths are coffee trust and footprint; weakness is matcha may be a menu add-on rather than the core brand.
- Dessert and beverage cafes: bubble tea, acai, soft-serve, juice, and dessert concepts that compete for cold premium beverage occasions.
- Japanese / Korean / lifestyle cafes: closer aesthetic competition and likely stronger matcha association.
- At-home matcha retailers and importers: compete for high-intent consumers but can become wholesale or kit partners.

**MajdaMatcha whitespace**

- A specialized matcha-first Saudi brand with credible sourcing and clear sweetness levels.
- Better cold-drink craft than chain matcha, with flavors built around local taste preferences.
- A retail and catering layer from day one, not just a cafe counter.

## Pricing Assumptions

Indicative pricing is based on Saudi specialty cafe norms, observed local menu examples showing espresso and flavored lattes around SAR 12-22, and premium hotel / destination venues reaching higher beverage prices. Final prices should be tested against exact rent, supplier quotes, and target gross margin.

| Item | Launch price assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Classic iced/hot matcha latte | SAR 22-26 |
| Flavored signature matcha | SAR 26-32 |
| Premium seasonal / dessert matcha | SAR 32-38 |
| Alternative milk upgrade | SAR 3-5 |
| Matcha dessert / cookie | SAR 12-22 |
| Home matcha tin, 30-40g | SAR 75-140 |
| Starter kit | SAR 180-320 |
| Event/catering drink package | SAR 18-28 per drink equivalent |

**Margin assumptions**

- Target beverage gross margin: 65%-75% after ingredients and packaging.
- Matcha powder cost risk is high because global demand has strained premium Japanese supply. Build menus around measured grams per drink, approved second suppliers, and seasonal price reviews.
- Delivery platform commissions can materially reduce margin; delivery prices may need a 10%-15% uplift or dedicated bundles.

## Regulatory & Operating Considerations

- Register the commercial entity and relevant municipal activity before trading.
- Obtain municipal / Balady licensing for the cafe, kiosk, or food truck format, plus Civil Defense approvals where applicable.
- Follow Saudi Food and Drug Authority food hygiene expectations, especially for imported food ingredients, labeling, storage, allergen handling, and traceability.
- Use a ZATCA-compliant POS and e-invoicing setup. ZATCA lists food and beverages at 15% VAT and e-invoicing applies to VAT-subject businesses.
- Confirm import route for matcha, dairy alternatives, packaging, and food-contact items; maintain invoices, certificates, Arabic labels where required, and batch traceability.
- Use staff training for cold-chain handling, dairy and nut allergen separation, cleaning schedules, and drink consistency.
- Plan Saudization, employment contracts, payroll, health cards / certificates, and local labor compliance with a qualified advisor.
- Check music, signage, outdoor seating, delivery, and event permits by location and municipality.

- Licenses, professional fees, insurance, deposits.
- Staff recruitment and training.
- Launch marketing, creator seeding, product photography, packaging.
- Working capital for 3-6 months.

## Revenue Model

**Core channels**

- Walk-in beverage sales.
- Delivery app sales and direct ordering.
- Dessert add-ons and bundles.
- Corporate/event catering.
- Retail matcha tins, kits, cups, and accessories.
- Limited seasonal drops and brand collaborations.

**Unit economics targets**

- Average order value: SAR 35-48 walk-in, SAR 45-65 delivery bundle.
- Beverage gross margin: 65%-75%.
- Dessert gross margin: 50%-65%.
- Retail gross margin: 40%-60%.
- Repeat purchase target: at least 2 visits per month among loyalty members by month 9.

## 12-Month Launch Roadmap

**Months 1-2: Validation and setup**

- Finalize Riyadh launch hypothesis, customer personas, brand positioning, and menu.
- Collect supplier quotes from Japanese and GCC importers; approve at least two matcha grades and two backup suppliers.
- Run blind tastings for classic, strawberry, rose, pistachio, and date/honey matcha.
- Confirm regulatory route, entity setup, POS / e-invoicing provider, and food safety plan.

**Months 3-4: Prototype and audience build**

- Launch creator tastings, office sampling, and private pop-ups.
- Test pricing, sweetness levels, cup sizes, packaging, and prep time.
- Build Instagram, TikTok, Google Business profile, and landing page waitlist.
- Secure first corporate/event partnerships.

**Months 5-6: Commercial pilot**

- Operate 8-12 pop-up days or a short residency in Riyadh.
- Launch delivery from a compliant prep location if licensing allows.
- Track sell-through, repeat orders, ingredient yield, customer reviews, and queue times.
- Decide whether to proceed with kiosk, shop-in-shop, or micro-cafe.

**Months 7-9: First permanent format**

- Open the first small permanent Riyadh location or shop-in-shop.
- Introduce loyalty, subscriptions, retail tins, and catering menu.
- Run seasonal limited drops tied to Saudi holidays, Riyadh events, and weather.
- Build operating manuals and staff training materials.

**Months 10-12: Optimize and prepare second market**

- Improve COGS, supplier terms, labor scheduling, and delivery packaging.
- Evaluate Jeddah pop-up or partnership launch.
- Build B2B catering pipeline with offices, gyms, salons, universities, and events.
- Decide on second site only if month 10-12 KPI thresholds are met.

## KPIs

**Commercial**

- Daily drinks sold by channel.
- Average order value.
- Gross margin by SKU.
- Delivery commission as percentage of sales.
- Revenue per labor hour.
- Break-even sales per day by format.

**Customer**

- Repeat purchase rate.
- Loyalty signups and active members.
- Google rating and review count.
- Net promoter score from QR receipt survey.
- Social saves, shares, tagged posts, and creator conversion.

**Operational**

- Drink preparation time.
- Waste percentage for milk, matcha, syrups, and desserts.
- Stockout days, especially premium matcha.
- Complaint rate by 100 orders.
- Staff training completion and recipe compliance.

**Decision thresholds for expansion**

- 3 consecutive months of positive store-level contribution margin.
- Beverage gross margin above 65%.
- Repeat customer rate above 25%.
- Average Google rating above 4.5.
- No single supplier responsible for more than 70% of critical matcha supply.
